Why more than three colors

Your eyes see three bands. Sentinel-2 records 13, and airborne sensors like AVIRIS-NG record around 425. Healthy leaves reflect a lot of near-infrared that we can't see, water absorbs almost all of it, and bare soil climbs slowly.

A lot of my geospatial work, from crop mapping to mineral mapping, starts with differences like these. NDVI is just the contrast between band 4 (red) and band 8 (near-infrared).

Hover or tap the chart to read each band. The curves are typical shapes for illustration, not measurements.

AGU Fall Meeting and UC Santa Cruz thesis

Assessing long-term changes in sea surface temperature extremes

I fit Generalized Extreme Value models to 36 years of global daily sea surface temperature (NOAA-OISST, 1982 to 2018) to see where the extremes are changing fastest. High latitudes won, especially the seas north of Russia and around Northern Europe. I also tested how the Arctic Oscillation and ENSO push those extremes around. Co-authored with my advisors Claudie Beaulieu and Katerina Giamalaki.

AGU Fall Meeting 2020 and MBARI

When do phytoplankton blooms start in the Subarctic Pacific?

One summer, three BGC Argo floats in the Gulf of Alaska, plus satellite chlorophyll. A recent hypothesis said blooms start in December or January. My results said March, which fits the traditional spring story. My best guess for the difference: that hypothesis came from the North Atlantic, and I was working in the Gulf of Alaska.
